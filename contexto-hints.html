Contexto is the word game that broke the "guess the letters" mould. Instead of Wordle-style green and yellow squares, Contexto asks you to guess a secret word and then ranks your guesses by semantic proximity — how close they are in meaning to the target. It's a game of vocabulary, intuition, and thinking in categories.

And it can be brutally hard. That's where we come in.

Every day we publish a fresh set of Contexto hints and clues — ordered from subtle to obvious — so you can get unstuck without having the answer handed to you. The full Contexto answer is always available, but hidden behind a spoiler toggle so you don't ruin it by accident.

What Is Contexto? (Quick Refresher)

If you're new to the game, here's the 30-second version:

  • You type any word you like.
  • Contexto tells you how close it is to the secret word, ranked by meaning.
  • If your guess is #1, you win.
  • Unlike Wordle, there are no letter hints — only semantic ones.

That means guessing "table" when the answer is "chair" is a good move. Guessing "table" when the answer is "ocean" is not. The game rewards you for thinking in concepts and contexts, not spelling.

Pro tip: Contexto's ranking system is based on AI word embeddings. Two words can be opposites and still be "close" if they frequently appear in the same contexts — e.g. "hot" and "cold" are neighbours. Use that to your advantage.

Contexto Strategy: How to Get Better at the Game

Hints are great, but the real fun is getting better at Contexto itself. Here are the strategies that consistently work:

1

Start broad, then narrow

Open with a category word — "food", "animal", "emotion", "object". Contexto will rank it, and you'll immediately know which semantic neighbourhood you're in. Then drill down.

2

Use opposites as probes

If "hot" ranks high, try "cold". If "big" ranks high, try "small". Opposites often cluster near each other in embedding space, so they can reveal the axis the answer sits on.

3

Think about word frequency

Contexto's answers are usually common-ish words. If you're guessing obscure technical terms, you're probably drifting. Try everyday words in the same category.

4

Track your rankings

Write down the ranks of your guesses. If "dog" is #45 and "cat" is #120, you're in the pet/animal zone. If "dog" is #800, you're way off. Use rank deltas to navigate.

5

Don't fixate on one guess

If a guess ranks poorly, don't keep tweaking it. Pivot to a completely different category. Contexto rewards broad exploration early, precision late.
