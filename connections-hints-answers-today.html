Theme Breakdown: Why These Groups Work

A strong theme breakdown teaches you to see tomorrow’s puzzle faster, so here is the reasoning behind each group.

Yellow: Steal

BOOST, PALM, POCKET and SWIPE are all informal verbs for taking something. ‘Palm’ and ‘pocket’ are about hiding an object in your hand or clothing, ‘swipe’ is a quick grab, and ‘boost’ is slang for stealing. Three of the four have other meanings, which is why it is smart to confirm the whole set before submitting.

Green: Pasta Shapes

BOW TIE, ELBOW, SHELL and WAGON WHEEL are all shapes you can buy in a box. Farfalle is the bow tie, macaroni is the elbow, conchiglie is the shell, and rotelle is the wagon wheel. Multi-word entries are a handy clue, because they usually belong to a single, specific group.

Blue: Fictional Toms

RIPLEY, SAWYER, SWIFT and THUMB complete the names Tom Ripley, Tom Sawyer, Tom Swift and Tom Thumb. Name groups are often easiest to find by saying the shared name aloud with each word, as in ‘Tom Thumb, Tom Swift’.

Purple: Things That Can Be Snapped

FINGERS, FOOTBALL, GLOW STICK and RUBBER BAND all take a snap. You snap your fingers, you snap a glow stick to activate it, you snap a rubber band, and a center snaps the football to start a play. The common action is the only link, which is why purple is the hardest group.

Red Herrings and Overlaps in Today’s Puzzle

Connections is a game of deliberate overlap. The designers build the board so that at least one tempting group is a trap, and today’s trap is easy to spot once you know it.

The decoy is body parts: PALM, THUMB, ELBOW and FINGERS all fit neatly together, yet each one belongs to a different group. That is four words, four categories, and no real body-parts group at all.

Three other words behave like chameleons. PALM is a tree, a body part and a stealing verb. SHELL is a pasta shape, a seashell, an eggshell and a fuel brand. SWIFT is a bird, an adjective for fast, a famous author’s surname and a fictional Tom. When a single word has three plausible homes, the safest move is to place the words around it first and let it fall into the last open slot.

Best Solving Order for Today

You do not have to solve in color order, and today’s puzzle shows why. A smart sequence uses the groups with the least ambiguity first.

  1. Start with Pasta Shapes. BOW TIE and WAGON WHEEL are unusual enough to point straight at pasta, and ELBOW and SHELL fall in line once you see it.
  2. Then Fictional Toms. With ELBOW and SHELL claimed, only THUMB, SWIFT, SAWYER and RIPLEY remain as plausible Toms.
  3. Now Steal. PALM, POCKET, SWIPE and BOOST are the four remaining verbs of taking.
  4. Finish with Things That Can Be Snapped. The leftovers, FINGERS, FOOTBALL, GLOW STICK and RUBBER BAND, fall into place without a mistake.

Notice that purple was solved last by elimination. That is a very common pattern. The hardest group is often easiest to complete when the other three are already out of the way.

Connections Strategy for Every Day

1

Look for the group you are sure of

Find four words you could defend in a sentence, not just three. Submitting a group of three plus a guess uses a mistake you may need later.

2

Scan for words with multiple meanings

Words like PALM, SHELL and SWIFT are the ones that cause most mistakes. Park them until the rest of the board is clearer.

3

Say shared words aloud

Name and phrase groups, such as Toms or ‘___ Wheel’, are often easier to hear than to see.

4

Use the ‘One away’ message

When the game says you are one away, three of your four words are right. Swap one word for a leftover that fits the same idea.

What the Four Colors Really Mean

Yellow, green, blue and purple signal increasing difficulty, but not increasing obscurity. Yellow is usually a straightforward meaning group. Green often needs a small leap, such as pasta shapes. Blue usually involves wordplay or a trivia link, like fictional Toms. Purple tends to be the most abstract, with a link that is less about the words and more about what you can do with them. Today follows the pattern almost perfectly, which is a good reason to guess the colors in order if you are unsure where to begin.

Connections FAQ

What are the NYT Connections answers today?

For Connections #1232 on October 25, 2026 the groups are: Steal (BOOST, PALM, POCKET, SWIPE); Pasta Shapes (BOW TIE, ELBOW, SHELL, WAGON WHEEL); Fictional Toms (RIPLEY, SAWYER, SWIFT, THUMB); and Things That Can Be Snapped (FINGERS, FOOTBALL, GLOW STICK, RUBBER BAND).

What is today's Connections yellow group clue?

Think pickpockets and burglars. The yellow group is about taking something that does not belong to you, and all four words work as verbs.

What is the hardest group in today's Connections?

Purple, Things That Can Be Snapped, is the trickiest because the four items have little in common except the action. FOOTBALL is the least obvious member, since the center ‘snaps’ the ball to start a play.

What is the red herring in today's Connections?

Body parts. PALM, THUMB, ELBOW and FINGERS look like a group, but each belongs to a different category: Steal, Fictional Toms, Pasta Shapes and Things That Can Be Snapped.

How many mistakes do you get in Connections?

You can make four mistakes. After the fourth wrong submission the game ends and reveals the remaining groups.

What do the Connections colors mean?

Yellow is usually the most straightforward group, then green, then blue, and purple is the trickiest. The order is a guide to difficulty, not a rule for the order in which you must solve them.

When does a new Connections puzzle appear?

A fresh puzzle unlocks at midnight in your local time zone, and every player worldwide gets the same sixteen words.
