BMI Categories.

Body Mass Index categories for adults aged 20 and older, as defined by the World Health Organization.

Category BMI Range (kg/m²) Health Risk

The BMI Formula

The Body Mass Index is calculated using one of two formulas depending on the unit system:

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²) Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²)
Questions

Frequently Asked Questions.

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a numerical value calculated from a person's weight and height. It is widely used as a screening tool to categorize whether an adult is underweight, at a healthy weight, overweight, or obese.

In metric units, BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²). In imperial units, BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height² (in²). Our calculator supports both systems and converts automatically.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO): Underweight is below 18.5; Normal weight is 18.5–24.9; Overweight is 25.0–29.9; Obese Class I is 30.0–34.9; Obese Class II is 35.0–39.9; Obese Class III is 40.0 and above.

BMI is a useful screening tool for most adults, but it has limitations. It does not distinguish between muscle and fat, so athletes may have a high BMI without excess body fat. It is also less accurate for the elderly, pregnant women, and children. Always consult a healthcare professional for a full assessment.

For most adults aged 20 and older, a healthy BMI range is 18.5 to 24.9. This range is associated with the lowest risk of weight-related health problems.

BMI Prime is the ratio of your BMI to the upper limit of the healthy range (25). A BMI Prime below 1.0 means you are within the healthy range; above 1.0 indicates overweight. For example, BMI 22.5 → BMI Prime 0.90.
