The Complete Guide to Converting Images to PDF

PDF is the universal format for documents that need to look the same on every device. Images are the universal format for capturing visual information. Bringing the two together — turning images into PDFs — is one of the most useful everyday digital tasks.

Our free online image to PDF converter lets you combine any number of JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, or BMP files into a single, well-formatted PDF. It runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript, so your images never leave your device.

What Does "Converting Images to PDF" Actually Do?

Converting is the process of taking one or more image files and embedding them into a PDF document. Each image becomes a page (or part of a page) in the output PDF. The PDF then contains the visual content in a standard format that can be printed, shared, archived, or sent to any platform that accepts PDFs.

  • Universal format: PDFs open on every device, without needing to know the original image format.
  • Multi-page support: A PDF can contain many images, one per page, in the order you choose.
  • Self-contained: All images are embedded — no external files to keep track of.
  • Lossless: Our converter embeds images at full quality. Visual content is preserved exactly.
  • Standard page sizes: PDFs can use standard paper sizes (A4, Letter) for printing, or match the image aspect ratio.

In short, image to PDF conversion is the digital equivalent of putting photos into an album — organized, shareable, and standard.

There are dozens of reasons to convert images to PDF. Here are the most common:

Page Size, Orientation, and Margin Explained

When you convert images to PDF, you have several layout choices that affect how the final document looks:

Page Size

  • Fit to Image: Each PDF page is sized exactly to match its image. No cropping, no margins. Best when preserving the image's aspect ratio matters most.
  • A4, Letter, A3, A5: Standard paper sizes. The image is scaled to fit within the page (with optional margins). Best for printing.

Orientation

  • Auto: Each page's orientation matches its image. A landscape photo gets a landscape page.
  • Portrait: All pages are portrait. Landscape images are scaled to fit inside a portrait page.
  • Landscape: All pages are landscape. Portrait images are scaled to fit inside a landscape page.

Margin

The margin controls the whitespace around each image when using a standard page size. Options range from None (image fills the page edge-to-edge) to Large (60 pt of whitespace on all sides).

Which Settings Should I Use?

  • Submitting documents: A4 or Letter, portrait, small margin.
  • Photo album: Fit to Image, no margin — each photo preserves its aspect ratio.
  • Presentations: Landscape, A4 or Letter, no margin.
  • Receipts & forms: A4, auto orientation, small margin.
  • Design portfolios: Fit to Image — never distorts or crops.

Tips for Better Image to PDF Conversion

Follow these tips to get the best results:

  1. Order images before uploading. Rename files with numeric prefixes (01-, 02-, 03-) so they appear in the correct sequence.
  2. Choose the right page size. For printing, use A4 or Letter. For digital sharing, use Fit to Image.
  3. Compress before converting if size matters. Large images make large PDFs. Compress images first if you need a smaller file.
  4. Use consistent image dimensions. If images in a PDF have wildly different dimensions, the pages will look uneven.
  5. Preview in your mind. Our converter lays each image on its own page. If you need multiple images per page, that's a different tool.
  6. Check the result. Open the PDF and scroll through to verify page order and layout.
  7. Name it well. Use descriptive filenames like "receipts-2024-q3.pdf" instead of "images-merged.pdf".
  8. Keep the originals. Conversion doesn't modify your source images, but keep backups anyway.

Common Image to PDF Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uploading sensitive images to unknown servers. Use a browser-based tool like ours to keep your images private.
  • Using low-resolution images. Printing a PDF built from tiny JPGs will look pixelated. Start with good sources.
  • Forgetting to reorder. Always check the visual order of your images before creating the PDF.
  • Mixing landscape and portrait carelessly. Use Auto orientation unless you have a specific reason to force one.
  • Overloading a single PDF. Very large PDFs (500+ pages) can be slow to open. Consider splitting into multiple PDFs.
  • Not previewing. Open the final PDF to confirm everything looks right before sharing.
