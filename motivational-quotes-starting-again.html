5 Motivational Quotes for Starting Again

Restarting is harder than starting, because this time you have evidence. Five short quotes for the second attempt, each explained, plus a ten-minute way to begin again.

The first time you start something, you have optimism. The second time you have evidence — a clear memory of how it went wrong. That is why restarting feels heavier than starting, even when the task is smaller.

The quotes below are chosen for that specific weight. Each one names a different reason people stall after a setback, and each points at a small first move. Read them one at a time and keep the one that fits.

Why Restarting Is Harder Than Starting

After a failure, three things quietly raise the cost of beginning again.

  • The failure is vivid. You remember exactly how the last attempt went, so the next one is judged against it before it begins.
  • The bar has moved. You now expect to be measured, which turns a simple first step into a performance.
  • You overcorrect. Instead of repeating the part that worked, you try to rebuild everything at once — and stall.

A good restart quote shrinks all three: it makes the next step small, ordinary and forgivable.

The 5 Quotes, Explained

Each quote below comes with a short explanation of why it works and where it is best used. Copy the one you need, skip the rest.

“

You miss 100% of the shots you don’t take.

Wayne Gretzky

The arithmetic is the point: not trying guarantees the zero. After a setback, the only move with any chance of a different result is another attempt. Best for the second try you are tempted to skip.

“

Success is a journey, not a destination. The doing is often more important than the outcome.

Arthur Ashe

Ashe moves your attention from the verdict on the last attempt to the act of doing. Best for when you keep measuring yourself against a result you have already lost.

“

Do not wait to strike till the iron is hot; but make it hot by striking.

W. B. Yeats

Readiness is a product of action, not a precondition for it. Best for anyone waiting to “feel like” restarting — the feeling usually arrives after the first ten minutes.

“

A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step.

Lao Tzu— Tao Te Ching

The oldest restart advice there is. It reduces the whole mountain to one step you can take today. Best when the scale of the comeback is what is stopping you.

“

It does not matter how slowly you go as long as you do not stop.

Confucius

After a failure, speed is the wrong metric; continuity is the right one. Best for the person comparing a slow second attempt to a fast first one.

Five quotes, five different stalls. Pick the one that names why you have not restarted yet — that match matters more than how well the line is phrased.

A restart is not a repeat of the failure. It is the first page of a different attempt.— WebNotePad Team

How to Restart in Ten Minutes

Reading a quote does nothing on its own. This short method turns one into a first step.

1

Name what actually failed

Write one specific sentence: “The launch slipped because I tried to build everything first.” “I failed” is not specific enough to act on.

2

Pick one quote

Choose the line that answers your stall — Yeats if you are waiting to feel ready, Confucius if you are comparing speeds.

3

Halve the scope

Write the smallest version of the attempt that still counts. Half the features, half the time, one page instead of ten.

4

Do step one now

Set a ten-minute timer and begin before you reconsider. Stop when it rings; you have already restarted.

Do this once and the note becomes a record of what changed between attempt one and attempt two — which is more useful than any quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good quote for starting again after failure?

Yeats’s “make it hot by striking” is the most practical, because it tells you not to wait until you feel ready. Confucius’s line about going slowly is the kindest for anyone who is rebuilding at a slower pace than before.

Who said “You miss 100% of the shots you don’t take”?

Wayne Gretzky is the best-known source of the line. He has said it came from his father’s advice, which is why it is often quoted as a family saying as much as a hockey one.

How do I start again when I have lost motivation?

Do not wait for motivation. Shrink the task to a ten-minute version, begin, and let the momentum arrive afterwards. Action usually produces the feeling rather than the other way round.
